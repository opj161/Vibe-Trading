# Bar-Boundary Sensitivity: External Research Synthesis and Updated Test Plan

**Purpose:** Analyze the four external deep-research reports (`Bar Boundary Sensitivity Analysis.md`, `bar-boundary-research-report-grok.md`, `Crypto Bar Boundary Robustness.md`, `bar-boundary-research-report-gpt.md`) run against the deep-research prompt from the prior session, cross-check them against each other and against our own empirical findings, and produce an updated, prioritized test plan. This supersedes the informal brainstorm from earlier in the session — that brainstorm is folded in and re-prioritized below using what the external research added.

**Context this builds on** (see `vibe_trading_research_findings.md` §15 for full detail): our EMA(10,30) + vol-targeting + risk-parity strategy on BTC/SOL shows Sharpe ranging from **-0.06 to +1.19** on identical price data purely from shifting the daily-bar UTC close hour. BTC is fairly stable across offsets (Sharpe 1.06-1.43); SOL is wildly unstable (-0.64 to +0.26). A 6-offset ensemble (`vibe_trading_research_findings.md` §17, "Variant N" — after fixing a lookahead bug in the first attempt, verified not to have affected the original offset-sensitivity finding) converges to roughly the *average* of the single-offset Sharpes rather than beating the best one: frozen OOS Sharpe 0.84 (vs. the single-offset "Variant L" result of 1.09), but with meaningfully better drawdown (-19.0% vs -30.6%) — a robustness/variance-reduction result, not free alpha, and probably a more honest expected-value estimate than L's single-offset number.

## 1. How much to trust these four reports

Before acting on any of this, an honest credibility assessment. All four reports converge heavily on the same core diagnosis and the same top recommendations, which is a meaningful signal — four independently-run tools landing on the same mechanism and the same "don't over-engineer the combination" caution is stronger evidence than any one report alone. But LLM deep-research reports are known to sometimes produce fluent, specific-sounding citations that don't fully check out, so citations were tiered by verifiability:

- **High confidence, independently verifiable, well-known outside these reports:** Lopez de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (2018) and its time/volume/dollar/imbalance bar taxonomy; Kaufman's Adaptive Moving Average (KAMA) and Efficiency Ratio; the Deflated Sharpe Ratio / Probability of Backtest Overfitting framework (Bailey & López de Prado); Corey Hoffstein / Newfound Research's "Timing Luck" body of work (a well-known, real, long-running research thread in the systematic-investing community); SG Trend Index, BTOP50, and other named CTA benchmark indices. These are all real, standard, checkable concepts — safe to build on directly.
- **Independently corroborated by us:** the arXiv paper "Systematic Trend-Following with Adaptive Portfolio Construction" (2602.11708, "AdaptiveTrend") that GPT's report cites with Sharpe 2.41 / 2022-2024 / 150+ pairs — we already fetched and read this paper directly in the prior session (see `vibe_trading_research_findings.md` §10-11) and confirmed those exact numbers. This is a strong, positive signal for GPT's report's citation accuracy specifically.
- **Plausible but individually unverified:** the December 2025 *Financial Innovation* paper on information-driven bars for crypto (Grądzki, Wójcik & Lessmann) — cited independently by *two* reports (Grok and GPT) with consistent but not identical detail (GPT's version is more hedged: "CUSUM bars beat time bars, but volume/dollar bars did NOT consistently improve" vs. Grok's more sweeping "strongly advocated... with supporting evidence"). Two independent tools citing the same specific paper is a good sign it's real; the *disagreement in how confidently to interpret its results* is itself useful information (see §3 below) and GPT's more hedged reading should be weighted more heavily.
- **Lower confidence / treat as illustrative, not load-bearing:** very specific numbers with no independent check possible — e.g. Report 3's liquidity-depth table ("$3.86M depth within 10bps" at 11:00 UTC, "$2.71M" trough at 21:00 UTC, attributed to "Amberdata and Talos") and its specific "348 basis points CAGR dispersion" claim. These aren't necessarily wrong, but shouldn't be treated as load-bearing facts without independent confirmation — use them as *directional* evidence (liquidity does vary by session; boundary choice does matter) rather than precise figures to design around.
- **Direct disagreement between reports, worth noting rather than papering over:** Report 3 claims peak crypto liquidity is at **11:00 UTC**; GPT's report cites a real, checkable paper (Brauneis et al. 2025, *Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting*, "The crypto world trades at tea time") finding the peak around **16:00-17:00 UTC**. These don't agree. This mirrors our own single-sample finding that 8h UTC had the best point-Sharpe (not 11h or 16h) — three different "best" answers from three different sources is itself the clearest evidence that **picking a boundary by which one looks best (in any single sample) is exactly the overfitting trap being warned about**, regardless of which specific hour someone points to.

**Bottom line:** the core diagnosis and the top two recommendations (ensemble = variance reduction not alpha; don't chase a "best" offset without ex-ante justification) are trustworthy and convergent across all four sources, including where we can independently check them. The more specific/exotic claims (precise liquidity tables, precise basis-point dispersions) should be treated as illustrative context, not facts to design a strategy around.

## 2. What the external research confirms (already knew, now grounded in real literature)

- **The phenomenon is real, not a bug in our test.** All four reports independently converge: this is a known class of problem spanning several formal literatures — *temporal aggregation bias* (econometrics, going back to Zellner & Montmarquette 1971, Amemiya & Wu 1972), *sampling-frequency sensitivity of technical rules* (Hudson, McGroarty & Urquhart 2017 — a real, checkable paper finding trend-following rules specifically deteriorate at higher sampling frequencies), and *Rebalance Timing Luck* (Hoffstein et al. 2019, a well-documented body of practitioner research). None of the four reports found one single canonical name for our *exact* setup (crypto, 24/7, EMA crossover, arbitrary offset) — it sits at the intersection of these, which is itself a useful finding: we're not chasing a phantom, but we're also not going to find one single paper that already solved our exact problem.
- **The mechanism (SOL fragile, BTC robust) is exactly what we hypothesized, now with a name and a formalism.** All four reports agree: higher realized volatility → more marginal/borderline EMA crossovers → more boundary-dependent whipsaws. Report 3 adds a specific, testable formalization via Rebalance Timing Luck: timing luck scales with strategy **turnover**, not just raw volatility per se. This is a sharper, more precise causal variable than "volatility" alone, and it's directly testable with data we already have (we already know SOL had 30-45 signal flips vs. BTC's 22-26 across offsets — turnover and volatility are correlated in our data, but formally testing turnover as the driver across *more* assets would sharpen this from "consistent with" to "confirmed").
- **The ensemble-converges-to-average result is exactly what should be expected, not surprising or disappointing.** All four reports derive this independently via slightly different but consistent math (portfolio variance formulas, correlation-of-signals reasoning). This means our corrected Variant N result (Sharpe ~0.84 OOS, roughly matching the offset-average rather than the best single offset) should be read as **the honest expected-value estimate of this strategy**, not as "we lost performance by fixing the bug." The earlier, uncorrected Variant L OOS Sharpe of 1.09 should now be understood as likely inflated by favorable timing luck on OKX's specific native offset — a genuinely important recalibration of how much to trust that number.
- **Realistic Sharpe benchmarks put our numbers in believable territory.** Multiple reports converge (with appropriately hedged numbers) on: traditional multi-asset CTA indices run ~0.4-0.7 Sharpe long-term; a "clean, diversified crypto trend-following backtest" Sharpe of 1.0-1.5 is plausible for a properly diversified system; concentrated 2-asset daily systems claiming Sharpe >1.0 should be treated skeptically as likely capturing some timing luck. Our corrected, offset-averaged Sharpe (~0.6-0.9 depending on exact measurement) lands credibly inside this range — reassuring in that our numbers aren't absurd, but also confirming L's originally-reported 1.09 was likely somewhat lucky rather than representative.

## 3. What the external research adds that we didn't already have

These are the genuinely new, actionable pieces of information beyond what we'd already derived ourselves:

1. **A concrete, simpler alternative to both "pick one offset" and "ensemble of discrete offsets": compute the EMA directly on intraday data with a calendar-time-scaled decay constant, never resampling to daily bars at all.** (GPT's report, item B.) Instead of "10 daily closes," define an EMA with a 10-*day* time constant evaluated continuously on the native 4H (or finer) series, then apply execution/turnover controls separately. This removes the arbitrary-boundary problem at its root for the *signal computation* step, while being far simpler to implement and reason about than a full alternative-bar-construction system. This is arguably the most promising single idea across all four reports that we hadn't already identified ourselves.
2. **A disciplined, testable starting hypothesis for asset-specific lookback scaling**, not just "make SOL slower" as a vague direction: scale lookback by *variance ratio*, not volatility ratio — `L_asset ≈ L_BTC × (σ_asset / σ_BTC)²`. For BTC vol ≈ 2.2% and SOL vol ≈ 3.6%, this gives a scaling factor of ≈2.7x, i.e. SOL's 10/30 becomes roughly **27/80** under this formula (GPT's report, explicitly flagged as "a disciplined hypothesis, not to be adopted blindly"). This gives us an actual number to test instead of an arbitrary "try EMA 20/60" guess — though per the report's own caution, and per our own experience this session (Variant J's blanket EMA15/45 change hurt because it slowed down BTC's *already-good* fast signal too), this must be applied **per-asset**, not uniformly.
3. **The empirical crypto-specific study is more nuanced than "alternative bars fix everything."** The one concrete empirical crypto study found by two of the four reports (Grądzki et al., Dec 2025) found CUSUM/information-driven bars outperformed time bars, but **volume and dollar bars specifically did NOT consistently improve results**, and are vulnerable to wash-trading distortion. This is an important corrective to the naive "just switch to dollar bars" instinct — the mechanism-appropriate fix is more specifically CUSUM/volatility-aware event sampling, not any alternative bar type indiscriminately.
4. **A name and existing statistical machinery for the "how much of this Sharpe is just from trying many offsets/variants" question**: the Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) framework (Bailey & López de Prado). This directly formalizes something we'd only informally worried about (repeated variant comparisons inflating apparent quality) and gives us an actual method to compute a corrected significance estimate, rather than just qualitatively flagging the risk as we have been.
5. **A clear, near-unanimous methodological warning we should take seriously given our own track record this session**: three of four reports explicitly warn against trying to extract "smarter" combination weightings from the offset ensemble (Sharpe-weighting offsets, meta-labeling, adaptive offset rotation) — calling it a "severe data-mining trap" (Report 3) or ranking it "dangerous unless validated with purged walk-forward testing and heavy shrinkage" (GPT). Given that in this same research arc, *every* attempt to add sophistication to the entry/exit logic (Donchian breakout, ATR trailing stop, trend-strength gate) backfired while simple approaches won, this external convergence is a strong, corroborating reason to resist the temptation to over-engineer the ensemble combination method.
6. **Session/liquidity-aware boundary choice is a real phenomenon (crypto does have intraday seasonality) but is a trap if backtest-driven.** GPT's report is the most careful here, citing two independently-real, checkable academic papers (Brauneis et al. 2025; Hansen, Kim & Kimbrough 2021/arXiv 2109.12142) establishing genuine crypto intraday seasonality in volume/volatility/liquidity — but explicitly states any session-based boundary choice must be justified **ex ante** (by liquidity/spread/funding-time reasoning) and never by "which offset scored best in my backtest," which is just a more sophisticated-looking version of the same overfitting trap. The disagreement between reports on the *exact* best hour (11:00 vs. 16:00-17:00 UTC vs. our own 8:00 UTC) is itself the strongest practical evidence for this caution.

## 4. Updated, prioritized test plan

This replaces the earlier informal brainstorm — same spirit, now sharpened and re-ordered using the external research. Grouped by theme, roughly ordered by priority within each group.

### A. Statistical rigor first (do this before trusting any further variant comparison)

1. **Compute Deflated Sharpe Ratio / PBO-style corrections** for the offset sweep and for the broader set of ~14 strategy variants tested this session. This directly quantifies how much of the apparent "best" results (single-offset Sharpe 1.19, or Variant L's 1.09 OOS) should be discounted for the number of comparisons made. This is the single highest-priority next step — it recalibrates everything else.
2. **Block-bootstrap confidence intervals around each offset's Sharpe** (the test already brainstormed pre-research) — now framed explicitly as a lightweight complement to (1), not a replacement.

### B. The most promising structural fix: continuous intraday EMA (new, from GPT's report)

3. **Implement and test an EMA computed directly on 4H (or finer) data with a calendar-time-scaled decay constant**, instead of resampling to any daily boundary at all. This is the most promising untried idea from the external research — simpler than full alternative-bar reconstruction, and it removes the arbitrary-boundary problem at the signal-computation root rather than averaging around it. Compare against both the single-offset and the ensemble-of-offsets results on the same OOS window.

### C. Asset-specific signal speed (sharpened from the earlier brainstorm)

4. **Test the variance-ratio-scaled SOL lookback** (~27/80 per GPT's formula) as a concrete, disciplined hypothesis, keeping BTC's already-robust EMA(10,30) untouched — apply per-asset, not uniformly (learn from Variant J's mistake).
5. **Test whether offset-sensitivity (Sharpe range across offsets) correlates with *turnover* specifically**, not just static realized volatility, per Report 3's Rebalance-Timing-Luck framing (`L ∝ turnover`). This sharpens the "why SOL vs BTC" causal story from "vol-related" to "turnover-related" if confirmed, which would directly justify signal-speed adjustments (fewer trades) as the fix, rather than just "slower" in the abstract.

### D. Broader asset sweep (from the earlier brainstorm, now with a sharper target variable)

6. **Extend the offset-sensitivity sweep to more assets** (ETH, BNB, XRP, ADA, LINK, AVAX, DOGE — several already have cached OHLCV from earlier universe-selection work) and **regress offset-sensitivity (Sharpe range, or Sharpe std across offsets) against both realized volatility AND turnover** (per item 5) to see which is the better predictor. This turns "BTC good, SOL bad" into a general, predictive asset-selection/parameterization rule.
7. **Denser offset grid (24 hourly instead of 6 four-hourly) on BTC/SOL** to check whether the sensitivity curve is smooth (real microstructure effect) or jagged (more consistent with small-sample Sharpe-estimation noise) — directly informs how much weight to put on item A's statistical corrections.

### E. Alternative bar construction (re-scoped, more targeted than the original brainstorm)

8. **Prioritize CUSUM/volatility-event-based sampling over blanket dollar/volume bars**, per the more nuanced empirical reading (item 3 in §3) — the one real crypto-specific empirical study found dollar/volume bars did *not* consistently help, while CUSUM-style did. This changes the original brainstorm's "volume/dollar bars as the structural fix" into a more targeted "CUSUM-style event bars," with lookbacks re-calibrated (not "10/30 bars" = "10/30 days" once bars are event-triggered).

### F. Session/liquidity-aware boundary (re-scoped with the ex-ante-only caveat)

9. **Only pursue a session-anchored boundary if justified independently of backtest performance** — e.g., by actual funding-rate settlement times (00:00/08:00/16:00 UTC on major exchanges — a real, mechanical, non-overfit-prone anchor unlike "whichever offset scored best"), not by picking whichever hour happened to show the best Sharpe in our own or any cited study. If tested, report it as one candidate among the full offset sweep, not cherry-picked after the fact.

### G. Re-examinations of earlier-session conclusions (carried over, still valid)

10. **Re-test the Donchian breakout signal (Variant D, rejected earlier) across the offset sweep** — it was rejected based on a single native-offset backtest; if it's *also* offset-fragile, that rejection may have tested an unlucky draw rather than fairly judging the breakout concept.
11. **Correlation of BTC's and SOL's flip-days** — shared (market-wide) vs. idiosyncratic (asset-specific) noise driver — determines whether a portfolio-level or per-asset robustness treatment is the more efficient fix.

### H. Discipline carried forward from the external research's clearest warning

12. **Do not pursue "smarter" offset-combination weighting** (Sharpe-weighted offsets, meta-labeling, adaptive rotation) without extremely rigorous purged walk-forward validation — three of four reports independently warn this is a data-mining trap, and it matches our own repeated finding this session that added sophistication to signal logic backfires. Equal-weight (or simple median/trimmed-mean) combination remains the recommended default unless a specific enhancement survives (1)'s deflated-Sharpe correction.

**If prioritizing a small number of next actions**: (1) Deflated Sharpe/PBO correction on everything tested so far, since it recalibrates how much to trust prior results; (2) the continuous intraday-EMA approach (§B), since it's the most promising untried structural fix and is simpler to implement than full alternative bar construction; (3) the broader asset sweep with turnover as the target variable (§D), since it converts an isolated two-asset observation into a general, predictive rule.
