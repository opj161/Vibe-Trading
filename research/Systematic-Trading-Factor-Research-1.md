# **Systematic Equity Factor Premia in the Post-2023 Regime: A Comprehensive Evaluation of Alpha Libraries**

The transition of a systematic trading research platform from cryptocurrency time-series trend-following to cross-sectional equity factor investing represents a profound paradigm shift. Cryptocurrency markets, historically characterized by extreme retail dominance, fragmentation, and pronounced time-series momentum, operate under distinctly different microstructural rules than traditional equity markets. Equities, conversely, are heavily institutionalized, driven by cross-sectional relative value, and tightly bound by macroeconomic regimes and complex derivatives ecosystems.  
The integration of approximately 456 pre-built cross-sectional equity factors—drawn from the WorldQuant Alpha101 family, the Microsoft Qlib158 repository, the Guotai Junan Securities GTJA191 set, and standard academic proxies such as the Fama-French and Carhart models—provides a robust foundational matrix for universe screening and signal generation1. However, deploying formulaic alpha zoos out-of-the-box in the current market environment invites severe adverse selection. Factor decay, structural shifts in market microstructures, institutional crowding, and the ubiquitous threat of multiple-testing bias render naive factor implementation highly hazardous3.  
This report provides an exhaustive analysis of the live, decayed, and evolving status of major equity factor categories as of the 2024–2026 market regime. The analysis delineates regional asymmetries across US large-caps, China A-shares, and Hong Kong equities; dissects the acute momentum-crash risks present in highly concentrated contemporary markets; and identifies the optimal multi-factor combinations currently demonstrating empirical resilience. Furthermore, the analysis establishes modern best practices for mitigating look-ahead bias and backtest overfitting—specifically utilizing the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) and Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)—and explores how the proliferation of zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) options and stubborn retail order flows have fundamentally altered the propagation of factor anomalies in the post-2023 era.

## **Current Live-Versus-Decayed Status of Major Factor Categories**

The landscape of equity factor premia has undergone a violent restructuring over the past 36 months, driven by shifting monetary policies, artificial intelligence (AI) capital expenditure cycles, and geopolitical fragmentation7. The empirical evidence drawn from 2024 through mid-2026 highlights a stark divergence between factors that continue to command live premia and those that have succumbed to publication decay, crowding, or structural impairment10.  
Price momentum and growth factors have emerged as the undisputed leaders of the 2024–2026 regime7. Driven largely by blockbuster earnings and aggressive capital expenditure in the mega-cap technology sector and broader AI infrastructure ecosystems, momentum strategies achieved their strongest performance since the late-1990s tech boom7. Over the 2024–2025 period, the top momentum quintile in the US outperformed the S\&P 500 by over 40%12. Within global universes, growth and momentum subfactors—particularly those tied to three-year EPS growth forecasts—consistently posted the highest positive excess returns, effectively neutralizing the drag of higher interest rates through sheer earnings acceleration7.  
However, this live premium is highly precarious. The momentum factor has become structurally entangled with extreme sector concentration10. Institutional researchers note that in long-duration growth sectors, returns have become hypersensitive to sentiment and momentum signals, largely ignoring defensive characteristics10. While the premium remains live, it is explicitly vulnerable to macroeconomic shifts, such as inflation persistence, tariff volatility, or a deceleration in AI monetization9.  
The value factor—often defined by book-to-market, earnings yield, or cash-flow yield—has experienced a highly localized and cyclical resurrection rather than a structural, global return to dominance7. Value has proven to be the strongest performing factor in European and Emerging Markets (EM) during the 2024–2025 period, thriving on policy easing, a weaker US dollar, and cheaper cyclical valuations7. Conversely, in the US large-cap universe, traditional value has materially lagged7. Brief periods of value outperformance in the US during early 2026 were deceptive; the gains were not driven by a broad re-rating of fundamentally cheap companies, but rather by the more expensive stocks within value indices experiencing multiple expansion12. Therefore, the value premium is deemed conditionally dependent on the regional universe.  
Quality (profitability, high return on equity, low leverage) and Low Volatility (min-vol, low beta) have faced substantial headwinds in the risk-on, AI-driven rallies of 2024 and 20257. Rising duration risk premiums, fading safe-haven demand, and an environment where investors eagerly paid a premium for growth caused defensive factors to underperform severely7. During the second quarter of 2025, Low Volatility logged some of the largest negative relative returns across global factor portfolios, dropping by nearly 20% in specific subfactor evaluations11. While these factors are not structurally eliminated, their standalone premia have been suppressed by the current interest rate plateau and pro-cyclical growth expectations8. Their contemporary utility is almost entirely derived from their role as portfolio stabilizers in multi-factor combinations rather than as standalone return drivers.  
The size factor (small-cap premium) has exhibited similar geographical divergence. In Europe and Emerging Markets, size made a strong comeback in 2025, supported by rate cuts and domestic demand sensitivity, positioning small caps as key beneficiaries of easing cycles and valuation discounts7. The MSCI Europe Small Cap strongly outperformed the broader European index. Conversely, in the US, the Russell 2000 severely lagged the S\&P 500 as capital clustered in large-cap entities with stable balance sheets7.  
Short-term reversal (buying recent losers and selling recent winners over a one-week to one-month horizon) features heavily in the Alpha101 and GTJA191 factor libraries1. In gross backtests, these formulaic expressions exhibit exceptionally high Information Coefficients (ICs) and Sharpe ratios. However, recent rigorous empirical examinations demonstrate that short-term reversal is experiencing a replication crisis5. The apparent premium is largely an artifact of two interconnected biases: correlated errors-in-variables (bid-ask bounce) and Look-In Bias (LIB)5. Factor backtests frequently use the closing price of a given day to both generate the signal and simulate the execution of the trade. When corrected for this implementation shortfall and realistic transaction costs, the short-term reversal premium drops by over 90%, rendering it economically insignificant for most institutional capacities5. Any formulaic alpha reliant on high-turnover mean reversion must be viewed as severely decayed or practically untradable without specialized, ultra-low-latency execution architecture.

## **Regional Asymmetries: China A-Shares, US Large-Caps, and Hong Kong Equities**

The pre-built factor libraries (GTJA191, Qlib158, and Alpha101) exhibit profoundly different efficacy depending on the applied universe. The efficiency of a market directly dictates the complexity required to extract cross-sectional alpha.  
The US large-cap space is the most hostile environment for simple formulaic alphas. Recent practitioner evaluations of the Alpha101 and Qlib158 libraries on the S\&P 500 reveal that virtually no single cross-sectional factor achieves a stable Information Coefficient greater than 0.02 out-of-sample18. Institutional coverage, high-frequency market making, and the dominance of quantitative hedge funds have effectively arbitraged away simple linear relationships18. In the US market, institutional density dictates that raw formulaic libraries function purely as raw feature inputs for highly complex, non-linear machine learning models rather than functioning as standalone trading signals.  
Conversely, the China A-share market remains highly retail-participant-heavy and structurally constrained by mechanisms such as short-sale restrictions and T+1 trading rules. This environment provides a much more fertile ground for cross-sectional anomalies. The GTJA191 library was specifically mined for this universe, and standard machine learning approaches using Qlib158 features routinely demonstrate high double-digit annualized excess returns16. Recent AI-driven agentic frameworks, such as AlphaAgent, running on the CSI 500 have maintained stable ICs between 0.02 and 0.025 and annualized alphas exceeding 11% net of costs over multi-year periods3.  
However, this alpha is punctuated by severe, episodic factor crowding3. Early 2024 witnessed a catastrophic collapse in the China A-share small-cap factor3. Direct Market Access funds and quantitative asset managers had become uniformly concentrated in identical small-cap factor structures. When a market shock induced sudden deleveraging, the crowded exit triggered a liquidity vacuum, destroying years of alpha in a matter of weeks3. Thus, while the A-share market supports live premia from formulaic libraries, it demands extreme vigilance regarding factor turnover and institutional crowding metrics. The platform must treat A-share anomalies as highly profitable but fragile.  
Hong Kong equities occupy an intermediate space on the efficiency spectrum. Characterized by high concentrations of financial, real estate, and technology conglomerates, the Hong Kong market frequently mirrors the value and size anomalies observed in broader Emerging Markets7. Because the Hong Kong universe lacks the hyper-liquidity of US large-caps but possesses more institutional participation than mainland A-shares, factors derived from fundamental valuation (e.g., book-to-price, dividend yield) and earnings quality tend to exhibit slower decay rates. The Qlib158 library, which heavily features fundamental and accounting-based metrics, is generally more robust in this region than the purely price-volume-based Alpha101 set.

## **Momentum Crowding and Crash Risk in the 2025–2026 Regime**

The current market environment presents a textbook setup for a severe momentum crash. While price momentum possesses over 150 years of empirical persistence across geographies and asset classes, its defining vulnerability is an extreme left-skewed return distribution, generating fat-tailed drawdowns that can decimate systematic portfolios22.  
Momentum crashes reliably occur under specific, converging macro-structural conditions, all of which are observable or developing in the 2025–2026 macro regime. First, during periods of high volatility, such as when the VIX spikes, recent winner stocks are usually the most crowded by institutional and retail capital, making them highly vulnerable to forced liquidation22. Second, momentum implicitly bets on trend persistence. In sharp market reversals—such as a sudden pivot from a growth-scare drawdown back to a risk-on rally—momentum portfolios hold short positions in the recent losers, which suddenly surge, and long positions in the recent winners, which face aggressive profit-taking22. Third, the current momentum premium is heavily concentrated in AI and mega-cap technology10. If these specific macroeconomic drivers, such as AI capital expenditure or accommodative liquidity, shift, the reversal will be exceptionally violent because the momentum signal lacks sector diversification10.  
For a systematic platform utilizing pre-built alphas, running raw twelve-month minus one-month (12M-1M) price momentum is currently ill-advised. To avoid the crash trap, modern implementations must utilize Risk-Managed Momentum constructs22. This requires implementing volatility scaling at both the individual stock level, through inverse volatility weighting, and the portfolio level, targeting a constant ex-ante volatility22. This mathematical constraint ensures that the system automatically deleverages during market stress. Furthermore, the formulation of the momentum signal must strictly exclude the most recent month of returns. The most recent month is heavily contaminated by the short-term reversal effect and microstructural noise; failing to skip it actively degrades the true momentum signal23. Blending price momentum with alternative momentum composites—such as fundamental momentum derived from earnings revisions or residual momentum stripped of market beta—significantly reduces the correlation to simple trend-following and has been empirically shown to halve maximum drawdowns22.

## **Superior Factor Combinations in Contemporary Markets**

Given the documented cyclicality of individual factors, the consensus across institutional researchers heavily favors multi-factor combinations over single-factor tilts. The objective of systematic portfolio construction is not to maximize returns in a single phase, but to smooth the equity curve and reduce the depth of drawdowns across varying economic regimes24.  
The most resilient combination backed by current evidence is the integration of Quality, Value, Momentum, and Low Volatility, frequently structured via index methodologies like the S\&P 500 QVML or MSCI Adaptive Multi-Factor indices24. Value and Momentum are structurally orthogonal and complementary. Value identifies statistically cheap stocks, but these assets often suffer from being value traps. Momentum acts as an empirical filter, identifying cheap stocks that have recently experienced a positive sentiment inflection, thereby confirming that the fundamental value is being recognized by market participants24.  
Historical analysis over a 150-year dataset demonstrates that no single factor leads in every inflation or monetary regime24. During moderate to mild-overshoot inflation, which characterizes the 2025–2026 baseline, momentum typically leads. However, if the regime abruptly shifts toward stagflation or deflation, size and value suffer immensely, while quality and low volatility stabilize the portfolio24.

The system must standardize the cross-sectional factor values for Quality, Value, and Momentum into z-scores, sum them, and sort the universe25. This integrated approach captures stocks exhibiting multi-dimensional strength, heavily outperforming mixed portfolios of isolated single-factor sleeves.

## **Implementation Pitfalls and Statistical Rigor**

Integrating 456 distinct factors from the Alpha101, Qlib158, and GTJA191 libraries creates an extreme susceptibility to data mining and backtest overfitting. A platform lacking strict statistical correction mechanisms will inevitably select spurious alphas that fail in live trading6.  
The Deflated Sharpe Ratio, developed by Bailey and López de Prado, is a non-negotiable requirement for a systematic platform of this scale6. The False Strategy Theorem dictates that when testing hundreds of formulaic variations, the expected maximum Sharpe ratio under the null hypothesis (that all strategies have zero true skill) increases logarithmically with the number of trials30. If a researcher tests 1,000 parameter combinations, a raw annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.5 possesses virtually zero statistical significance. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects for this by adjusting the significance threshold based on the number of independent trials, the non-normality of the returns (incorporating skewness and excess kurtosis, as fat tails artificially inflate traditional Sharpe estimates), and the variance of the trials6. To implement this natively, the backtester must track every single run—including failed iterations—to accurately estimate the effective trial count and compute the probability that the best observed Sharpe ratio exceeds the mathematically expected maximum generated purely by noise6.  
Similarly, the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) quantifies the likelihood that the strategy performing best in-sample will rank below the median out-of-sample29. The platform must support Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV), dividing the historical data into an even number of blocks, forming all possible in-sample and out-of-sample combinations, and computing the performance degradation matrix. A factor combination with a PBO greater than 0.5 is effectively a coin toss and should be discarded regardless of its gross Sharpe ratio29.  
Beyond statistical overfitting, the two most lethal traps for formulaic libraries are Look-In bias and the failure to account for market impact5. Many academic datasets align financial fundamentals to the end of the fiscal quarter, ignoring the reporting lag. A point-in-time database is mandatory; a fundamental signal must only be visible to the backtester on the exact timestamp the financial filing was made public32. Furthermore, calculating a signal based on the closing price of day zero and executing the trade at the closing price of day zero assumes time travel5. The backtester must strictly enforce a next-day open or volume-weighted average price execution logic. Finally, high-turnover factors exhibit severe capacity limits. Transaction costs, including bid-ask spread, exchange fees, and market impact scaled to the square root of order size relative to average daily volume, degrade short-term alphas exponentially17. Any backtest failing to explicitly deduct realistic transaction costs is generating fictitious profitability32.

## **AI-Era Market Structure: 0DTE Options and Retail Flows**

The microstructure of global equities has fundamentally shifted post-2023. The assumptions underpinning classic factor anomalies—primarily that institutional arbitrage slowly corrects mispricing over months—are being challenged by the dominance of zero-days-to-expiration options and retail order flows33.  
In the US, 0DTE options now account for approximately 50% of the total daily volume in the S\&P 50035. Initially, widespread fear suggested that the massive gamma exposures inherent in these ultra-short-dated options would cause a mechanical increase in market volatility as dealers scrambled to hedge. However, rigorous empirical research from 2024–2026 demonstrates the exact opposite: 0DTE options mechanically dampen realized index volatility36. The mechanism is driven by pre-existing inventory. Option market makers accumulate positions in longer-dated options that slowly roll down the term structure until they become 0DTEs on the day of expiration. This accumulated net gamma exposure forces dealers to trade against contemporaneous intraday price movements to remain delta-neutral36. Consequently, intraday momentum factors are frequently suppressed or rapidly mean-reverted, leading to severe pinning behavior near the market close as hedging flows converge36. Systematic platforms trading intraday or very short-term multi-day horizons must account for these dealer-gamma barriers, as traditional technical breakouts often fail against the weight of options hedging.  
Concurrently, a massive influx of retail options trading, facilitated by payment for order flow and zero-commission brokers, has altered the decay rate of anomalies33. Retail flow is generally uninformed, highly concentrated in lottery-like out-of-the-money options, and persistently trades against established academic anomalies33. Groundbreaking econometric research concerning "Stubborn Retail Investors" fundamentally rewrites the understanding of factor decay34. Conventional wisdom posits that as markets become more efficient, factors disappear. However, analysis of hundreds of anomalies reveals that anomalies heavily traded against by retail investors have never disappeared41. Instead, the stubbornness of retail traders—who persistently inject noise and mispricing into the market—forces arbitrageurs to absorb substantial long-horizon risk41. Because arbitrageurs require compensation for this risk, the alpha of these anomalies is preserved, but it materializes over an extended 24-month horizon rather than the traditional one-month formation period. In recent empirical studies, these specific anomalies yielded staggering value-weighted two-year alphas of 23%41. For the backtester, this implies that factors exploiting retail behavioral biases should utilize significantly longer holding periods to capture the true risk premium.

## **Final Judgment: The Virtue of Sparsity in Complexity**

In addressing the final requirement regarding the single most important finding not directly solicited by the prompt, the focus must shift from *which* factors to trade to *how* a platform should process a 456-factor library. The most critical paradigm shift in modern quantitative asset pricing is defined in recent literature as the "Virtue of Sparsity in Complexity"42.  
Historically, researchers operated under the assumption that introducing hundreds of factors into a model invited catastrophic overfitting, thus favoring parsimonious, low-dimensional models like the Fama-French framework. The modern realization is that limiting the dimensionality of the candidate feature space—known as capacity sparsity—is actively detrimental to alpha generation42. True economic value is unlocked through factor sparsity applied within a regime of massive complexity.  
By utilizing nonlinear feature expansions—taking the 456 factors from Alpha101, Qlib158, and GTJA191 and calculating their interactions, polynomials, and cross-products to create thousands of features—and subsequently applying rigorous machine learning regularization such as basis pursuit or L1-Lasso shrinkage, the model evaluates an enormously rich candidate space42. Complexity does not compete with sparsity; it is the mathematical prerequisite for it. A massive feature space allows the machine learning algorithm to select the exact, sparse pricing kernel that actually drives returns in the current market regime42.  
The implication for the platform is profound. The objective should not be to backtest individual factors to find the best five. Instead, the architecture should ingest all 456 factors simultaneously, expand them nonlinearly, and apply strict L1 regularization or deep learning architectures to dynamically shrink the noise and extract the latent sparse pricing kernel. This methodology routinely dominates traditional linear multi-factor portfolios out-of-sample, fundamentally resolving the tension between the vast size of the pre-built factor zoo and the need for robust, generalizable trading signals.
